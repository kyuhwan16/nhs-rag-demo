Title: The Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) and NHS Access

International students on a Student visa for longer than six months are usually required to pay the Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS) as part of their visa application. Once paid, this gives the student the same access to NHS services as a UK resident, including free GP visits and free or subsidised hospital treatment.

The IHS does not need to be paid again separately when registering with a GP; registration itself is free regardless of IHS payment, but the IHS affects entitlement to wider NHS services such as hospital care.

Some services remain chargeable even for those who have paid the IHS, such as dental care and optical care, which typically require a separate co-payment similar to that paid by UK residents.

Confusion commonly arises among new international students about whether they need private health insurance in addition to the IHS; in most cases, the IHS is sufficient for standard NHS care during a Student visa, and separate private insurance is not required for routine treatment.
